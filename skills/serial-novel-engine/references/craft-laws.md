# Craft laws (free edition selection)

Each law came out of something that went wrong in a finished 200–360 chapter serial.

## Planning
1. **Plan the whole book before Chapter 1.** Every chapter has a row. "Continue" means
   draft from the plan, never invent the next arc.
2. **Give the act a countable clock.** Vague menace pulls nothing; "fifteen days" pulls hard.
3. **Plan the anti-sag spine past Chapter 30.** The reveal ladder and the act climaxes are
   that spine.
4. **Fuse two engines per chapter** (romance + mystery, revenge + survival). A chapter
   running on one engine gets skimmed.
5. **Familiar outside, new inside.** Keep a searchable hook, but list the genre's default
   beats for that hook and break at least three with a mechanic the reader hasn't seen.
   Combine two tropes that rarely share a book. Readers notice when a story is a renamed outline.

## Curiosity
6. **Every chapter opens or sharpens a question the reader is dying to know.** That's a
   feeling you put in the reader, not a question a character says out loud.
7. **Every question you open is a debt.** Touch it every few chapters until you pay it.
   Carrying too many at once (more than about ten) is what readers call "dragging".
   Pay old threads off before opening new ones.
8. **Tell someone a secret exists without telling them the secret.**

## Character
9. **Don't let the love interest solve the protagonist's problem.** He may open a door;
   she walks through it or she doesn't.
10. **Let her cause the disaster by doing the right thing**, and show her doing everything
    right before you knock her down, so the fall reads as theft rather than comeuppance.
11. **The best villain attack is one she can't answer because it's true.**
12. **Vary the kind of win inside a winning streak**, and pair a victory chapter with the
    one that undercuts it.
13. **Confess in units of time, not adjectives.** Not "I've known a while" but "ninety
    days", counted so the reader could have done the arithmetic.
14. **A refused apology is stronger than an accepted one.**

## Production
15. **Draft long once.** Aim the first pass at the top of the band (~50 paragraphs, 12+
    spoken exchanges). Drafting short and patching costs more than drafting long.
16. **Recompute every running clock from the anchor day at the top of each batch.**
    Check "N weeks since" against the day count, never against chapter numbers.
17. **Coin minor names on the page and log them the same session**, and check new names
    against the whole cast list. Late-act characters collide with early ones.
18. **The AI fingerprint and the dragginess are the same problem**: em-dashes, aphorism
    scaffolding, reflective padding. Cutting them is retention work, not polish.
19. **No script checks voice or register.** A human editor reads every batch.
