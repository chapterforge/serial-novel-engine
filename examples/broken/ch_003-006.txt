===CHAPTER 3===
The Second Page

"Read it first," Sam said. "Then ask."

I read it. Then I read it again, because it was short and because it couldn't say what it said.

"She couldn't do this," I said.

"She could, and she did. I drafted it."

"Who picked the seventeenth?"

He looked at me steadily. "Jonah Marsh rang this office on the first of May and asked how long probate would take."

===CHAPTER 4===
What the House Remembered

The house had always held its breath in the evenings, and that night it held it longer than usual. I walked the length of the upstairs corridor and thought about my grandmother, and about the years she had spent in these rooms, and about what it meant that she had planned all of this without telling any of us.

The truth is, I had never known her as well as I believed. I had learned that grief arrives in rooms you thought were safe, and that it stays there, quietly, long after the people have gone.

I thought about the will, and the chapel, and the date, and I thought about how little time fifteen days really is when you are counting them one by one.

===CHAPTER 5===
The Long Night

I did not sleep. I sat by the window and watched the orchard go grey and then black, and I thought about my sister, and about the ring, and about the man in the navy suit who had known all of this for two months.

The thing about families is that they keep their secrets in plain sight. I would come to understand that much later, but that night I only knew that something in the house had changed, and that it would not change back.

By morning I had decided nothing, and I went downstairs as if nothing had happened.

===CHAPTER 6===
Terms

Sam was waiting at the chapel door — a testament to how seriously he took a deadline.

"It's a yes," he said. "With terms."

A wave of relief hit me, palpable and sudden, and the air between us hung heavy in the air of the little chapel.

"Name them," I said.

"Nobody knows it's an arrangement. Not Clara, not your father."

His eyes were impossibly dark — two pools of something I could not name.

"And the third term?"

"I ask you one question before Thursday," he said, "and you answer it honestly."
